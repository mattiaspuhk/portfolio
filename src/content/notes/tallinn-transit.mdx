This one is early. Nothing is built yet. A friend of mine, a senior engineer, and I are in talks with Tallinn's transport board (Liikuvusamet) about access to ridership data. I'm writing it down now because it's the project I'd most like to see exist.

### The idea

Every time you tap your travel card in Tallinn, the system records it. Riders never see any of that. The app would give it back to them: how many rides you took this month, the lines you use most, the stop you board at most often.

A light game sits on top. You collect the lines you've ridden, keep a streak going, and get a summary of your year at the end. It should make you notice how much you already ride. It shouldn't push you to ride more than you need to.

### Why open source

It isn't a business. There's no ads, no paid tier and no plan to sell the data. We want to give something back to the city, and open code fits that. Anyone can check what happens to their travel data, and another city with a similar card system could run its own copy.

Ride data is personal, so consent and privacy have to be agreed with the city before any code touches it. That is most of what the current talks are about.

### What it could become

- **For riders:** a clear picture of how they get around, and a small reason to keep choosing the bus or tram.
- **For the city:** an anonymised, opt-in view of how people ride, from people who chose to share it.
- **For other cities:** a working starting point instead of an empty repo.

I'll post an update once the data question is settled.
